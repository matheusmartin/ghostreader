# Writing style

All text in a guide follows The Economist Style Guide. This page sums up the rules that matter for
screens and diagrams. For anything else, follow the guide's own entry (its website has the
current edition). `npm run validate` checks the rules marked [checked].

## Capitals

- [checked] **Sentence case for every label** (screen labels, takeaway labels, section titles): "Key
  concepts", "Who is where", "Why it matters". No title case ("Story Threads").
- Every item, step, node, note and caption starts with a capital; the rest is lower case except
  proper names: "Falls for her", "At the dance hall", "Home in Brooklyn".
- Titles of the book, its parts and chapters keep the book's form ("Part I · Human Networks").
- Capitals for names of people, places, organisations and historical periods; lower case for
  general words (the company, the party, the north of the city). When unsure, use lower case.
- Text set in capitals by the app (labels, book map) is still written in sentence case.

## Punctuation

- [checked] No full stop at the end of labels. No full stop after notes, node names, chart labels or step
  text either; full sentences (In short, Why it matters, scene texts, synopses) end with one.
- [checked] No full stops in Mr, Mrs, Ms, Dr, St: "Mr Darcy", even when the book writes "Mr. Darcy".
- Chapter references: "Chapter 7" where there is room, "Ch 7" in a tight label. Never page numbers.
- No comma before "and" at the end of a list, unless an item contains "and".
- Dashes: a spaced en dash for a break ( – ), at most one pair per sentence; an en dash without
  spaces for ranges (Chapters 1–4). "·" separates parts of a short label ("Part I · Start").
- Quotation marks: double; single only inside double. Type straight quotes: the app sets them as
  typographic quotes. Guides do not quote the book, so quotation marks are rare.
- Possessives: 's after singular names that end in s (Jones's), s' after plurals.

## Numbers

- Words for one to ten, figures from 11: "three brothers", "23 chapters".
- Figures with units, money and percentages: $20, 4%, 15km.
- Never start a sentence or a label with a figure: "Seven days alone", not "7 days alone".
- Dates: month, day, year, no commas: October 18th 2004. Decades: the 1920s.
- Centuries: first to tenth in words, then figures: the 11th century.

## Spelling

- [checked] British spelling: colour, honour, centre, defence, grey, travelled, ageing, among (not
  amongst), while (not whilst).
- [checked] -ise and -isation: realise, organise, organisation, recognise.
- Names keep their own spelling (a company called "Center" keeps it).

## Plain words

- Short, common words: let not permit, buy not purchase, show not demonstrate.
- Cut every word that adds nothing. Active voice. One idea per sentence.
- No clichés or stale metaphors; no puns in labels.
- People are named as the book names them, without full stops in titles; after the first
  mention, the form the book uses most ("Elizabeth", "Mr Darcy").

## On a screen

- Labels: two to four words. Node names and notes: as short as the book allows.
- The point first: a summary says what happens or what the author claims, then why it matters.
- One idea per sentence, one idea per screen.
