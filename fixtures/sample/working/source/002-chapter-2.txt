# Chapter 2

[p1] Ada worked through the night. At dawn she came down the stairs with a small cloth bundle and opened it on the kitchen table.
[p2] Inside were grains of white salt. She had found them packed into the teeth of the main wheel.
[p3] Salt would not stop a clock by chance, she said. Somebody had opened the case with the key and put it there.
[p4] Ines knew that only two keys existed: the one her father carried and the one he had left with her.
[p5] She asked Tomas whether he had touched the key. After a long silence he admitted that he had lent it to Mr Hale for one evening, three days before.
[p6] Mr Hale had told him he needed to check the tower for the lighthouse board, and had given him a silver coin.
[p7] Tomas showed her the coin. He said he had not known what Mr Hale meant to do.
[p8] Ada cleaned the wheel, oiled it and set the hands. At noon the clock struck the hour, and the lamp began to turn again.
[p9] In the harbour, the three boats came in one after another. People on the quay cheered, and somebody rang the church bell.
[p10] Mr Hale came up the hill in the afternoon. He thanked Ada for her work and asked Ines for the key, saying the board wanted it kept at the harbour office.
[p11] Ines told him that her father would decide that on Friday.
[p12] When he had gone, she took the key from her pocket and hid it inside the hollow base of the lamp in the kitchen.
[p13] Ada packed her tools. She said she would stay in Kessel until the church clock was mended, and that she would keep the salt.
[p14] Ines sat by the window until dark, watching the light turn and wondering why the harbour master wanted her father out of the tower.
