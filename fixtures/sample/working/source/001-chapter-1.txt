# Chapter 1

[p1] The clock in the lighthouse at Kessel stopped on the first cold morning of the year, a little before six.
[p2] Ines Varga noticed it because the lamp did not turn. Her father kept the light, and the light kept to the clock: when the clock struck the hour, the lamp began to turn, and every captain on the coast knew it.
[p3] That rule was older than the tower. People in Kessel called it the keeper's rule, and they said a keeper who broke it would lose the tower.
[p4] Her father was away at the fishing grounds until the end of the week. He had left the key to the clock room with her and told her to touch nothing.
[p5] She climbed the stairs anyway. The clock face was cold, and the hands stood at ten minutes to six.
[p6] Her younger brother, Tomas, followed her up with a lamp. He said the clock had been ticking when he went to bed.
[p7] Ines said that somebody must have stopped it. Tomas looked at the floor and said nothing.
[p8] At the harbour, Mr Hale, the harbour master, was already angry. Three boats waited outside the bar, and none of them would come in without the light.
[p9] He told Ines that the town would write to the lighthouse board. If the clock was not running by Friday, her father would lose the tower and the house that went with it.
[p10] Ines asked him who in Kessel could mend a clock. Mr Hale said there was nobody, and that this was her family's problem, not his.
[p11] On the way home she passed the post office, where a notice said a clockmaker would arrive on the evening boat from the city.
[p12] The evening boat was late, because there was no light to guide it, and it came in under oars.
[p13] The clockmaker was a tall woman in a grey coat. Her name was Ada Moreau, and she had come to Kessel to repair the church clock.
[p14] Ines asked her to look at the lighthouse clock first. Ada said she would, for a price, and named a sum that Ines could not pay.
[p15] Ines offered her mother's brass compass instead. It was the only valuable thing she owned.
[p16] Ada turned the compass over in her hand for a long time. She said it was worth more than the work, and she agreed.
[p17] Tomas, who had come to the quay with his sister, asked Ada how long the repair would take. Ada said she could not tell until she had opened the case.
[p18] Mr Hale watched them from the door of his office. When Ada walked past, he asked her whether she had come about the church clock, and she said yes.
[p19] He did not mention the lighthouse.
[p20] That night Ines gave Ada the key to the clock room. It was the first time anyone outside the family had held it.
[p21] From the window of the house, Ines watched the clockmaker's lamp climb the stairs of the tower, window by window, until it reached the top.
